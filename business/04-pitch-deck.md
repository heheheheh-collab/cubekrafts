## Slide 2 — The Problem

### Buying a modular kitchen outside the metros is a lose-lose.

- **Overpay the brand** — Livspace/HomeLane charge **25–45% more** than local factory-direct dealers for the *identical* Hettich/Hafele hardware.
- **Or gamble on a carpenter** — no fixed quote, no comparison, no spec standard, no recourse if it goes wrong.
- **No middle option** — outside the top metros there is no *trusted AND affordable* way to buy.
- **A ₹1.5–8 lakh, once-a-decade purchase** made on a leap of faith.

**Speaker note:** "The buyer's choice today is 'expensive and safe' or 'cheap and scary.' Nobody owns 'affordable and trusted.'"

---

## Slide 3 — Why Now

### Three forces open the window.

- 🏙️ **Tier-2/3 is where the growth is** — rising incomes and new home completions, but incumbents are stuck in metros because their CAC is too high to expand down-market profitably.
- 🔩 **Hardware democratized** — Hettich, Hafele, Ebco now reach local factory-direct dealers, so a Tier-2 dealer can deliver brand-grade quality at 20–30% less.
- 📱 **Buyers already search** — "modular kitchen price [city]" is high-intent and, in Tier-2, *not* saturated by the incumbents' ad spend.

**Speaker note:** "The demand and the supply quality both exist in Tier-2. What's missing is the trusted layer connecting them."

## Slide 9 — Moat

### Trust + dealer lock-in, not code.

- **Dealer enablement lock-in** → their catalog, pricing, and quotes live on our tool; switching cost rises over time.
- **Local supply liquidity** → 3+ vetted dealers per Tier-2 city is hard to replicate city-by-city.
- **Standardized kitchen spec + price-transparency data** → apples-to-apples comparison and a pricing corpus that compounds.
- **Verified-dealer trust layer** → ratings from completed kitchens.

*Anyone can clone a calculator UI. No one can clone city-level dealer liquidity + the tooling lock-in.*

**Speaker note:** "Defensibility is the two-sided liquidity per city and the dealer tooling they won't want to leave."

## Slide 11 — Go-To-Market

### Supply-first, then cheap Tier-2 demand.

1. **Seed supply (concierge)** — hand-recruit factory-direct dealers; free to list, pay-per-qualified-lead.
2. **Manufacture early demand by hand** — route real buyer inquiries to prove value.
3. **Turn on scalable demand** — SEO + ads on **"modular kitchen price [Tier-2 city]"**, off the metro keywords incumbents own. Calculator = lead magnet.
4. **Sell the SaaS** — once dealers see lead value, upsell the quoting/catalog tool.

**Speaker note:** "Supply is cheap to acquire and *wants* leads. Demand is cheap because we deliberately avoid the keywords the incumbents bid up."
