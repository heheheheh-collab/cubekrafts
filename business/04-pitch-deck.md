## Slide 2 — The Problem

### India's local kitchen dealers are losing deals they should win.

- **Quotes on Excel & paper** — slow, inconsistent, often sent days late.
- **Mispricing** — guess the bill-of-materials wrong and the dealer eats the loss or loses the job.
- **Leads leak** — walk-ins and WhatsApp enquiries fall through the cracks; no follow-up.
- **No catalog** — they borrow PDFs and can't show finishes/options professionally.

**Speaker note:** "There are tens of thousands of these dealers. They're capable fabricators running a 1990s back office."

---

## Slide 3 — Why Now

### The window is open.

- 🏙️ **Tier-2/3 kitchen demand is booming** — branded players are stuck in metros (CAC too high to expand down).
- 🔩 **Hardware democratized** — Hettich/Hafele/Ebco now reach local dealers; quality is there, the software isn't.
- 📱 **Dealers are still on WhatsApp + Excel** — the workflow gap is wide open and no one owns the dealer relationship.

**Speaker note:** "Supply quality has caught up. The missing layer is the software that runs the dealer's business — and the demand that feeds it."

## Slide 6 — The Wedge

### Lead-gen-led. We earn the right to monetize.

**Most Indian SMB SaaS dies charging a subscription for a "nice-to-have."** We don't.

- **Money-in first** — free leads make us a revenue source, not a cost.
- **Then indispensable** — once their catalog + pricing live on us, we run their back office.
- **Then we monetize the transaction** — not their patience.

*ICP: growing 2–10 person dealers losing deals to slow quoting — NOT one-man carpenters.*

**Speaker note:** "We flip the usual SMB-SaaS failure: we bring money before we ask for it. That's how you beat the 'why pay, WhatsApp is free' default."

---

## Slide 7 — Business Model

### Transaction-attached. The opposite of the Khatabook trap.

| Line | Mechanic |
|---|---|
| **Free** | High-intent leads + basic quoting — acquisition hook, zero friction |
| **Per-won-deal fee** | We charge when the dealer *wins* a deal we helped quote/source |
| **Hardware-procurement margin** | Aggregate dealer hardware demand → margin on Hettich/Hafele/Ebco supply |
| **(Later) financing/EMI take** | Buyer financing on big kitchens → take-rate |

*Subscriptions, if any, are a thin add-on — never the core. Petpooja-style: mission-critical tool + transaction revenue.*

**Speaker note:** "Indian micro-SMBs convert 5–10% on subscriptions. We monetize the transaction instead — aligned with the dealer's own upside."

**Incumbents compete *with* dealers for homeowners; hardware brands give catalogs, not workflow; the dealer's actual back office is Excel.** The dealer-OS slot is empty.

**Speaker note:** "Livspace lost ~₹131 cr on ₹1,400 cr fighting for the homeowner. We're not in that fight — we arm the thousands of dealers they ignore."

## Slide 9 — Moat

### Lock-in + transaction data, not code.

- **Tooling lock-in** — catalog, pricing rules, quote history, and lead pipeline all live on us. Switching = re-entering their whole business.
- **Per-tenant pricing corpus** — we learn real BOM costs and win-rates city by city; the data compounds.
- **Procurement aggregation** — the more dealers, the better our hardware terms; a flywheel.
- **Act-2 marketplace optionality** — dealer catalogs already on-platform = consumer comparison with zero cold-start.

**Speaker note:** "Anyone clones a quoting UI. No one clones thousands of dealers' live catalogs, pricing, and the procurement flywheel."
